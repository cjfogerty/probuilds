Situation in One Page

MasterBrand is the #1 North American residential cabinet manufacturer. The May 28, 2026 all-stock merger with American Woodmark created the industry’s most comprehensive portfolio spanning stock, semi-custom, and premium. Pre-merger MasterBrand shareholders own ~63%. The combined company targets ~$90M run-rate cost synergies by year three and accretion in year two.

Channel mix (pre-merger MasterBrand): ~55% Dealer, ~32% Retail, ~13% Builder. Dealers remain the highest-margin, most controllable growth engine. The West Territory (TX, CA, AZ, CO, NM, UT and adjacent) contains both high-growth Sun Belt/Mountain West corridors and affordability-challenged California markets.

2026 is a transitional year: end markets expected down mid-single digits, tariff costs elevated (gross tariff exposure cited at 5–6% of sales before mitigation), and integration work is the commercial priority. Long-term structural drivers (demographics, underbuilding, R&R) remain intact.

★ The Five Imperatives for the West AVP

  1. Dealer Share & Portfolio Penetration — Convert the expanded brand set into placements and share of wallet with the existing MasterBrand dealer network.
  2. Builder & Retail Continuity + Mix Guardrails — Protect service levels and volume relationships while refusing pure volume-at-any-cost deals.
  3. Mix, Pricing & Margin Discipline — In a soft market, mix shift and price realization are the primary path to regional P&L success.
  4. Team / Agency Performance + Integration Execution — High-accountability field system; zero service friction during brand and process integration.
  5. West-Specific Resource Allocation — Prioritize TX/AZ/CO growth corridors; tailor California approach to design intensity and longer cycles.
Bottom Line for Leadership: The West can deliver outsized value in the next two quarters by treating the merger as a commercial offensive in the dealer channel, enforcing mix and pricing discipline, and running a tight integration cadence. Volume will be constrained by the market; profitable share and operational reliability will not.

Strategic Priorities — Competitive & Performance Bios

Five focus areas with competitive context, success metrics, and execution guidance.

Highest Leverage Dealers remain the highest-margin, most controllable channel. Post-merger, the commercial prize is placing the expanded portfolio (including AWM brands) into MasterBrand’s larger, denser dealer network where historical overlap was limited.

Competitive Reality

Cabinetworks competes hard on mid-tier brands and designer programs. Value importers win on speed and price in entry segments. MasterBrand’s advantage is breadth (stock → premium), service infrastructure, and now the ability to offer former AWM lines through the same sales motion.

Performance Metrics

  • Share of wallet / brand penetration with top 50–100 West dealers
  • % of dealer revenue from semi-custom + premium
  • New brand / display placements (especially AWM lines)
  • Agency and Territory Manager productivity
  • Dealer NPS / retention and competitive displacement wins

West Actions

  • Segment dealers A/B/C and resource accordingly
  • Rapid training and incentive alignment on full portfolio
  • Win showroom and specification share with priority partners
  • Use digital configuration tools to improve conversion and order quality

Builders and retailers provide volume and plant utilization. American Woodmark brought stronger builder heritage (including turnkey elements). The combined company must maintain continuity while refusing margin-destructive volume.

Competitive Reality

National and large regional builders value reliability, lead times, and package simplicity. Retail programs are concentrated and promotional. Importers and private-label pressure is highest at the value end. Service friction during integration is the largest near-term risk.

Performance Metrics

  • Revenue and share with priority West builder accounts
  • Retail program compliance, fill rates, category standing
  • Margin by channel (protect or improve vs. pure volume)
  • On-time / complete delivery performance

West Actions

  • Secure service continuity commitments with key accounts
  • Introduce higher-mix options into builder packages where feasible
  • Maintain disciplined pricing posture with retailers
  • Leverage expanded footprint for logistics reliability across the West

P&L Critical In a soft demand environment with tariff pressure, volume alone will not deliver regional goals. Mix shift toward semi-custom/premium and clean price realization are the primary levers.

Competitive Reality

Value competitors will fight on price and lead time. MasterBrand’s multi-brand architecture enables trade-up pathways that pure value players lack. Domestic/nearshore capacity is a relative advantage in a tariff environment.

Performance Metrics

  • Gross and contribution margin by brand, channel, and key customer
  • Mix percentage (semi-custom + premium)
  • Price realization vs. list and vs. prior year
  • Discount / promotion leakage

West Actions

  • Weekly price/mix visibility at regional level
  • Align agency incentives to margin, not revenue alone
  • Push configuration tools that surface higher-value options
  • Segment service levels to protect cost-to-serve

The AVP’s primary leverage is through Regional Sales Managers, Territory Managers, and independent agencies. Integration success or friction will surface first in the field.

Competitive Reality

Talent and high-performing agencies are contested. Competitors are also investing in commercial leadership. Zero service disruption is both a defensive requirement and an offensive differentiator.

Performance Metrics

  • Agency and manager attainment vs. target
  • Revenue and margin per sales resource
  • Forecast accuracy
  • Integration milestones (training completion, cross-sell activity, service levels)
  • Team engagement / retention

West Actions

  • Clear scorecards and weekly/bi-weekly operating rhythm
  • Accelerate post-merger product and process training
  • Surface integration issues upward quickly while protecting customers
  • Develop bench strength in high-growth sub-markets

The West is not uniform. Resource allocation and playbooks must differ by sub-region.

Sub-Region Guidance

  • Texas & Arizona: Volume and production-builder opportunity; value competition intense — defend with service and trade-up.
  • Colorado / Mountain West: Growth and mix potential; invest in design-oriented dealers.
  • California: Higher design intensity, longer cycles, stronger sustainability and customization expectations — prioritize high-potential design partners.

Performance Metrics

  • Relative growth vs. local housing starts, existing-home sales, and remodel indicators by major metro
  • Share trends in priority metros
  • Lead-time and logistics performance by sub-region

2-Quarter Execution Playbook

Concrete cadence for Q3 2026 (Aug–Oct) and Q4 2026 (Nov–Jan 2027). Designed for soft demand + active integration.

Q3 2026 — Stabilize, Train, Launch Cross-Sell

Weeks 1–4: Foundation

  • Finalize West dealer segmentation (A/B/C) and priority account list
  • Complete portfolio training for all agencies and RSMs on expanded brand set
  • Establish weekly integration + service dashboard (escalations, lead times, complaints)
  • Align agency incentive elements to mix and new-brand placement goals

Weeks 5–8: Offensive Launch

  • Execute top-50 dealer joint business plan reviews with full portfolio story
  • Secure first wave of AWM-brand display and specification wins
  • Lock service continuity commitments with top builder and retail accounts
  • Stand up regional price/mix weekly review with Sales Ops

Weeks 9–13: Embed & Measure

  • First formal scorecard readout vs. Q3 targets
  • Agency performance calibration; territory adjustments where needed
  • Document competitive wins/losses and feed back to product/commercial leadership
  • Confirm Q4 resource allocation by sub-region

Q4 2026 — Accelerate Share, Protect Margin, Close Strong

November: Momentum

  • Second-wave dealer penetration and cross-sell push
  • Holiday / year-end promotional discipline (protect mix)
  • Builder pipeline lock for early 2027 starts

December–January: Close & Reset

  • Full Q3–Q4 scorecard and variance analysis
  • 2027 territory planning input (priorities, agency design, investment asks)
  • Celebrate and communicate integration commercial wins to the broader organization
  • Identify residual service or process gaps for Q1 2027 resolution
Operating Rhythm Recommendation: Weekly 30-minute West leadership huddle (bookings, service flags, competitive intel) + bi-weekly deep dive with agencies/RSMs on priority accounts + monthly full P&L and scorecard review with Commercial leadership.
